Statistical Interpretation — Income Tracking

Methodology: One-Factor ANOVA (Treatment Effects Model). Raw yields showed non-normality confirmed by Q-Q plots and histogram skew. Levene's Test returned p < 0.05, confirming heteroscedasticity. A log1p transformation was applied prior to Welch's one-way ANOVA, which is robust to unequal variances.

Key Finding: Sectors with log-means above the grand mean — Real Estate, Utilities, Energy, Consumer Defense, Financial Services — offer superior cash-yield profiles relative to the broader S&P 500 universe. These sectors return more immediate income per dollar invested than average.

Stock-Level Insight: MSFT provides the best compensation for risk taken (Sharpe ≈ 0.77, ROI ~25.9%). PPL is the conservative anchor with lowest volatility (~21.9%). HBNC carries high risk (~32.5%) but delivers lower ROI (~10%), making it the least efficient on a risk-adjusted basis.

Statistical Interpretation — Management Efficiency

ROE/ROA Non-Normality: Q-Q plots for raw ROE and ROA show heavy tails and significant deviation. Histograms are sharply right-skewed — a few high-performing outliers pull the mean from the median. This justified Kruskal-Wallis and the log1p transformation.

Heteroscedasticity Persistence: Levene's Test failed (p < 0.05) for all three metrics. Financial Services and Technology show wider residual dispersion than Utilities. The GLS model with varIdent directly addresses this by allowing sector-specific residual variance.

Profit Margin Hierarchy: Technology and Financial Services sit significantly above the grand mean. Consumer Cyclical and Energy fall below, reflecting higher cost-of-goods-sold and capital-intensive operations. Utilities show tight error bars — highly regulated, predictable margins.

Future Returns Regression: The robust regression (rlm) was applied due to extreme skewness in earnings growth. Sector membership is a significant moderator — a one-size-fits-all momentum strategy is not valid across all GICS sectors.

Statistical Interpretation — Beta & PEG Ratio

Beta Distribution: Raw values cluster around 1.0 but extreme outliers create heavy tails in Q-Q plots. Log transformation normalises spread though financial kurtosis persists. Levene's Test failed (p < 0.05), confirming that high-beta sectors are also high-variance sectors — validating Welch's ANOVA choice.

Sector Sensitivity Hierarchy: Technology and Consumer Cyclical sit above the grand mean beta — they amplify market moves. Utilities and Consumer Defense fall below, providing downside buffering. Welch's ANOVA confirms sector membership is a primary driver of stock volatility.

The grand mean line acts as the market's average cost of growth benchmark.

Statistical Interpretation — Markov Chain Analysis

System Dynamics: The 10-step projection reveals a strong long-run drift toward churn-dominated states. Inactive_Churned becomes the most dominant state (~46%), indicating weak recovery or reactivation mechanisms in the current system. The Markov chain behaves as a churn funnel with increasing risk over time.

State Trajectory Summary: Active_Retained declines from ~38% to below 10% — customer retention is not sustainable without intervention. Inactive_Retained decays to near-zero rapidly — it is a transient state with a short intervention window. Active_Churned shows a consistent upward trend, indicating early-stage churn risk growing over time.

Risk Segmentation Mapping: States map directly to portfolio risk profiles: Active_Retained = Conservative (stable), Inactive_Retained = Moderate (intervention window), Active_Churned = High Risk (early warning signals), Inactive_Churned = Aggressive (churned, requires reactivation capital).

Business Implications: Protect Active_Retained customers first. Target Inactive_Retained quickly — this window closes fast. Monitor Active_Churned for early warning signals. Invest in reactivation pathways from Inactive_Churned (currently <5% recovery probability).
